# Case studies — what famous names teach

Loaded on demand during Stages 2–4 to calibrate judgment. Organized by the principle each case teaches, not by company. Sources: public reporting, company histories, and Wikipedia, cross-checked 2026-10.

## 1. A great name snapshots the product's birth moment

The origin story embeds itself when the name records what the product *was* at launch.

| Name | Decode | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| 抖音 | 抖 + 音 — the launch killer feature was music-synced shake effects | Name = feature demo; suggestive, verb-able |
| 饿了么 | The exact sentence users said when ordering campus takeout | The user's own words become the brand — zero education cost |
| 小红书 | Launched as a red-cover PDF《出境购物攻略》; the booklet became the brand | Artifact-to-brand continuity, warm and visual |
| 知乎 | Classical interrogative 知乎 — "do you know?" in one word | Question-culture encoded in two characters; literary yet functional |

## 2. Survivorship warning: storyless names win too

| Name | Decode | Teaching point |
|---|---|---|
| Spotify | No romantic story — reportedly a misheard/mangled riff during brainstorming; backronym "spot + identify" was built after | A mediocre name with a winning product still wins. Name quality raises odds of recall and registration, not of success. Never grade a giant's name as proof of method |
| Slack | Chosen for the colloquial "give you some slack"; the "Searchable Log of All Conversation and Knowledge" backronym is a joke added later | Post-hoc lore is normal; don't demand fake-deep origin stories from candidates |

## 3. The name is a legal asset — blood lessons from Chinese tea-shops

| Case | What happened | Rule extracted |
|---|---|---|
| 皇茶 → 喜茶 | 皇茶 boomed but couldn't stop copycats (un-registrable); 2016 founder spent ¥700k buying the 喜茶 trademark and renamed mid-flight | A name you cannot register is a name you're renting from counterfeiters; if blocked mid-flight, buying a distinctive new mark beats fighting |
| 鹿角巷 | Trademark filing left until one month before mainland launch; squatters filed thousands of variants; "7000 鹿角巷 stores, none are mine" | File before public launch, not after. The better the name, the bigger the squatting swarm |
| 茶颜悦色 vs 茶颜观色 | Near-clone 茶颜观色 was registered by a squatter who then sued the original; also squatted in Korea | Near-variants get registered against you — defensively file your own variant ring; and near-match warfare is why Stage 4's near-match query exists |
| 茶颜悦色 itself | 和颜悦色 idiom with 茶 swapped in — instant familiarity + category signal | Idiom modification (成语改造) is a high-yield move for consumer brands: preloaded meaning, distinctive enough to register |

## 4. Descriptive names can be saved by time, but they pay for it

| Name | Decode | Teaching point |
|---|---|---|
| 支付宝 | Descriptive: 支付 + 宝, spectrum-weak at birth | Decades of use bought it "acquired distinctiveness" — the spectrum grades initial protectability, use can earn protection back. Only viable when backed by an ecosystem and a long horizon; a startup should not plan on this rescue |
| Xerox | Coined from xerography | Same lesson in reverse — it started empty and was filled by decades of being the only one |

## 5. Verb-ification is the summit — never rename away from it

| Name | Decode | Teaching point |
|---|---|---|
| Google | Accidentally-misspelled googol (10^100 — a mission statement in a word); the misspelling made it unique and ownable | Score "can it become a verb?" (google it, 百度一下) as a bonus dimension; verb-potential names are brand moats |
| Twitter → X | Twitter (bird-chatter onomatopoeia) had grown a whole verb ecosystem (tweet, retweet) and was torched for a single letter | Renaming away a verb-built brand burns compounding assets; blocker for any rename rationale |

## 6. CN-EN decoupling beats translation

| Pair | Decode | Teaching point |
|---|---|---|
| 抖音 / TikTok | Not a translation — an onomatopoeic global coinage with zero pronunciation barriers | When going global, default to sound-native coinage or pinyin, never literal translation |
| 小红书 / rednote | Domestic brand kept, global audience given a new handle | Two names, two jobs: one carries the home story, one carries the world's mouth |

## 7. Borrow from everywhere — but vet the phonetics

| Name | Decode | Teaching point |
|---|---|---|
| Nike | Greek goddess of victory; Starbuck from Moby Dick's first mate; 夸克 from the physics particle | Borrowed proper nouns come with preloaded stories — cheap meaning, if the sound survives your market's mouth |
| Sony | Latin "sonus" (sound) + "sonny boys" slang mashup | Cross-language roots can combine if the result is short, bright, and empty of local obscenity |

## 8. Colloquial-meat CN moves: 叠字 and 谐音

| Name | Decode | Teaching point |
|---|---|---|
| 拼多多 | Verb 拼 + triple reduplication; carries its own slogan "拼得多，省得多" | Reduplication lowers conversational guards and sounds like a person, not a company; pairs perfectly with a built-in jingle |
| 闲鱼 | 咸鱼 ("lying flat" movie-culture slang) homophone-shifted to 闲 | A name that contains its users' inside joke gets shared for free |

## 9. Compounds are era-dependent

| Name | Decode | Teaching point |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube / Netflix / Snapchat | Category-explaining compounds that named new categories at birth | When the category itself is new, compound clarity is genius; in a mature category, compounds read as slop (see Stage 3 filter) — judge compound strategy against category age |
